# Linen beyond the ticket: maintain the service people intend

Proposed 27 September 2026. This extends the flagship destination in
[CONTRACT.md](CONTRACT.md). Implementation and selected provider evidence are tracked
in [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md); full release acceptance and measured service performance
remain separate gates. E1–E7 remain the complete employee release
floor. The new ambition is to continuously maintain intended service outcomes,
including discovering and engineering work that no existing runbook describes.

The existing plan already includes parallel diagnosis, preventive changes and
owner-authored connectors. More ambitious intelligence changes the unit of work:
from a case someone opened to a persistent commitment Linen is responsible for
keeping. A ticket is one way a broken commitment becomes visible. Useful work can
start before a ticket, continue after restoration and remove future demand.

The owner's RER thesis and capability premise warrant sustained, unfamiliar
research and engineering as a
normal product capability. Mathematical achievements motivate stronger reasoning
and checkable models; they do not establish empirical service recovery. Cheap
semantic decisions make it practical to observe more of the organisation, while
frontier teams can investigate dependencies and create missing capabilities.
Neither role imposes a permanent ceiling on a model or requires a particular brand.

## Seven extensions that change the product

### F1. Persistent service commitments

An owner says: “Every scheduled starter can do their first job”; “Ex-contractors
lose expired privileges while retaining separately valid access”; or “Order entry
remains usable through the declared identity-provider failure.” Linen turns each
into a versioned service commitment: affected people/workflows, meaningful checks,
time obligations, dependencies, permissible changes and the owner of exceptions.
It proposes the missing service design, including recovery arrangements, rather
than just configuring a ticket SLA. The owner settles business tradeoffs; agents
carry out the resulting work within granted scope.

This adds a durable responsibility beyond case closure. Event-driven work and
scheduled checks maintain it across staff changes, releases and previous repairs.
Linen shows the commitment's outcome, threatened scope and accountable exception
in the existing conversation or a small decision view. A service graph and
diagnostic detail open only when useful; no perfect digital twin is assumed.
Persistent context does not mean a permanently running model per asset.

### F2. Semantic observation and immediately useful help

Observe authorised changes to people, service configurations, supplier notices,
work patterns and employee reports. Combine these with deterministic monitors to
notice meaning: a starter's role changed after provisioning, a supplier retirement
threatens a dependency, or nominally healthy SSO no longer permits order entry.
Independent atomic questions can share a scoped evidence snapshot and run in
parallel. Fast models are candidates for these decisions; code handles filtering,
identity, deduplication, retrieval, schedules and unchanged-state suppression.

The employee sees a message in the existing conversation: “Your order service is
affected; this permitted alternative is available.” Relevant help appears without
another explanation or a new agent dashboard. Fresh evidence and current authority
determine what can be offered. Response speed is an experience goal to measure end
to end, not an untested promise based on inference latency.

### F3. Unfamiliar causal research

When the service model stops explaining observations, assemble scoped research
teams: identity, application, deployment and dependency specialists can pursue
rival explanations concurrently. Each supplies predicted observations and a
safe discriminating probe, not just a persuasive diagnosis. A coordinator chooses
probes for expected information and service value, preserves disagreements and
updates the causal model from results. Sustained investigation can span many
attempts and previously unfamiliar systems, reconstructing undocumented services
from source, configuration, API behaviour and authorised probes. Unknown coverage
remains visible as the service model improves.

This extends parallel troubleshooting into discovery. Missing observability can
become an engineering task. Serious signals, unfamiliar changes and coverage gaps
can start deep work directly; an erroneous cheap classifier must not gate every
route. Fast assistance and deep research can proceed at the same time. Scope,
stopping conditions and spending follow the commitment and remaining uncertainty.

### F4. Autonomous service engineering

An investigation may need a diagnostic tool, connector, application patch,
configuration redesign or different operating process. Linen creates the missing
capability through the existing [engineering lane](ARCHITECTURE.md#owner-agent-extension-lane),
with a reproducer, source change, independent checks, versioned release and actual
adoption. It can replace a brittle integration, simplify an entitlement model or migrate an
obsolete internal service. Changes belong in the actual owned service repositories
through the RER engineering path, preserving their source custody.

Separate standing engineering grants can cover development, testing, canary and
release: there need not be a human click for each preauthorised change. Such a
grant does not let the service executor approve its own permission expansion.
The existing A1–A7 contract governs the work. Product integration uses normal
edition-owned modules and releases, not a universal agent-building framework.

### F5. Executable service models and experiments

Translate useful parts of a service commitment into executable checks, state
machines and, where worthwhile, formally checkable properties. For example, under
explicit identity and timing assumptions, verify that expiry removes only a
particular grant's entitlements; or model-check that retry/takeover cannot admit
two conflicting release coordinators, or declare offboarding complete with an
outstanding revocation. A counterexample becomes a concrete repair.
Agents can derive better protocols and tests as well as implement known ones.

Use an experiment/replay environment to compare competing interventions and
exercise rare interleavings, with recorded inputs, source versions and uncertainty.
A replay reconstructs observed events; it cannot fill in unobserved dependencies.
Proof checks establish the stated model's properties. Provider semantics, sessions,
networks and usable employee work still require empirical checks. Measure whether
formal work avoids more failure and investigation than its creation/upkeep costs.

### F6. Turn solved cases into reusable capability

After a verified solution, produce a versioned package containing its detector,
applicability conditions, diagnostic questions, permitted action, verifier,
regression cases, provenance and known counterexamples. Repeated cases can then
use deterministic checks and measured cheap decisions, invoking deeper reasoning
only where needed. This adds an executable capability to the knowledge article
and known-error record already in the plan.

Test on held-out repetitions and deceptive near-matches before ordinary adoption.
Bind applicability to provider/action/model/context versions and evidence freshness;
changed facts invalidate affected decisions and trigger revalidation. A familiar
symptom with a different cause must open investigation instead of replaying an
old repair. Count discovery, failed experiments, packaging, maintenance and
invalidation in the economics of future reuse; cheap calls alone prove nothing.

### F7. Continuously simplify the service estate

Use evidence across commitments to find the sources of unnecessary work:
fragmented entitlements, brittle dependencies, redundant software, needless
approval steps, duplicative handoffs or an onboarding process that recreates the
same access failures. Agents develop and compare service redesigns, perform
permitted migrations and measure whether the demand actually disappears.

Agents compare changes by employee work preserved, failure exposure, service cost
and owner attention; no scheduled portfolio review is required. A recommendation
can prefer removing a fragile bridge to automating its repair forever. The owner
chooses genuine business compromises and changes outside standing authority;
authorised improvements continue automatically.
This is IT service design across people, suppliers and software, not an assertion
that Linen controls HR, finance or the entire company. Ticket throughput can fall
while success improves because the need for those tickets has been removed.

## The early demonstration: a promise survives an unfamiliar regression

Extend [Journey 4](UX.md#journey-4-a-detected-or-reported-service-outage) from
reactive recovery into the following connected demonstration. This is a proposed
scenario, not a claim about an existing provider or an already diagnosed failure.

1. **Promise.** A service owner records that the selected sales cohort can start
   a new session, find an account and prepare an order through a declared identity
   change. The UI shows coverage, observation age and existing engineering grants.
2. **Notice.** A permitted canary on a real selected stack fails after a change,
   even though component health is green. Linen relates the workflow observation
   to the release and opens work before waiting for an employee complaint. The
   conversation shows who is affected and what is unknown; scoped diagnostic
   detail opens for the operator who owns an exception.
3. **Discover.** Parallel teams test identity claims, application compatibility,
   regional routing and a supplier dependency. A plausible coincident deployment
   is challenged with a discriminating probe. Suppose the actual cause is an
   unfamiliar interaction between rotated claims and an old integration cache.
4. **Build what is missing.** No existing tool distinguishes the hypotheses.
   An engineering agent adds a scoped diagnostic and a reproducer, then creates a
   patch or connector change. A separate verifier tests the reproducer, fresh and
   cached sessions, unaffected roles and the incorrect alternative explanation.
5. **Recover.** An existing engineering grant permits an independently checked
   canary and bounded release. New-session and order-workflow observations verify
   the affected cohort; failed subsets remain open. Retain rollback and unknown
   effects under ordinary execution semantics. No real customer order is created
   as a probe unless that effect is explicitly authorised.
6. **Learn.** Generate a reviewed, tested detector/action/verifier package from
   the solved case. A later held-out instance with matching applicability uses the
   cheaper path. A deliberately similar failure with a different cause fails the
   applicability check and starts new research instead of receiving the old patch.
7. **Remove demand.** Compare the sustained cost of this compatibility bridge
   with simplifying the service integration. Implement a worthwhile granted change
   and observe whether comparable exposure produces fewer failures and less work.

The before state is a desk waiting for reports and repeatedly coordinating a
repair. The proposed after state is a service commitment that detects a breach,
invents missing engineering, verifies recovery and makes future operation simpler.
Access expiry/revocation remains the first fundamental connected evidence; this
second demonstration begins alongside it, rather than waiting for desk parity.

## Practical build sequence

| Step | Bounded outcome and parallel work | Evidence needed to advance |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Make the promise tangible | Add commitment/coverage and research-to-repair states to all six UX journeys; independently identify one real service and observable safe workflow | Employee/operator/owner walkthrough plus proposed effort budgets and explicit unknowns; retain E1–E7 experience work |
| 2. Connect observation | In parallel with temporary-access proof, connect one service commitment, selected events and end-to-end verifier; evaluate fast atomic questions beside capable-model baselines | Real observation/readback and staleness behaviour; observed all-attempt quality/latency/cost |
| 3. Complete unfamiliar repair | On an authorised representative environment, exercise the regression journey, missing diagnostic and independent canary verification | Exact change/effect/usable-work evidence; explain competing causes and remaining coverage |
| 4. Prove reuse and limits | Package the solved case; test held-out repeat and novel near-match, version drift, restart and affected policy/protocol property | Useful reuse, correct rejection of extrapolation and checkable property with stated assumptions |
| 5. Reduce continuing work | Complete E1–E7 in parallel, then broaden commitments and test one service simplification against a declared baseline | Employee release evidence plus sustained outcomes and full preparation/maintenance economics |

Selected providers, real principals and grants are build inputs. Missing ones
leave a connected gate pending while UX, source design and controlled tests
continue. [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md#frontier-extension-evidence) specifies the
additional evidence; no new latency target, saving or product acceptance is claimed.

## What Jev changes, and what remains to measure

Primary sources checked 27 September 2026. TypeSafe's 15 September announcement
presents Jev as a structured-decision model without string generation and claims
70–500 ms responses. This is vendor evidence, not Linen latency or proof of ITSM
quality; the cited sources do not establish a parameter count or that Jev is simply
a smaller LLM. [Announcement](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev).

Choice selects among finite alternatives, Score evaluates a rubric, and Noul
estimates a proposition's probability. Independent questions share state and run
in parallel; compose atomic decisions in ordinary code. Suitable Linen experiments
include evaluating whether a fresh fact contradicts a service assumption or
whether a known repair's semantic applicability conditions are met.
[API introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction).

Choice/Score confidence summarises the returned distribution; Noul has no separate
confidence field. Neither schema compliance nor confidence establishes correctness,
calibration for this task or authority. Missing/invalid output is no decision,
never an invented result. [Confidence documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence).

Route work by measured whole-outcome quality, latency and cost. Fast and deep
models can exchange roles as evidence changes. Include retrieval, queues, provider
calls, checks and communication in end-to-end latency; compare direct capable-model
routes as well as decomposed paths. No classifier is an obligatory front door,
no model is assigned to every log line, and no provider benchmark is a release gate.
